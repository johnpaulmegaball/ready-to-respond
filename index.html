In a 2025 survey of 1,006 Hawaiʻi households, 56% believed they were prepared. Only 12% actually had 14 days of water, food, and medicine. Believing it and doing it are not the same thing.

Why Hawaiʻi Is Different

🚢

Almost everything on the island arrives by sea. About 3,000 tons of food and 400 shipping containers land at Honolulu Harbor every day, and there is no alternate harbor that can unload freight at that scale.

🏬

There is no warehouse backup. Food moves straight from the port to stores, so when ships stop, shelves drain within days. After 5 days without imports, stores are below 40% of normal stock.

📅

FEMA’s minimum is 3 days of supplies. That number was built for the mainland, where trucks can roll in from the next state. Hawaiʻi has no next state. That is why HI-EMA recommends 14 days instead.

What 2 Weeks Ready Actually Looks Like

  • 14 days of water at 1 gallon per person per day
  • 14 days of nonperishable food and a manual can opener
  • 14 days of any prescription medication
  • A battery or solar powered radio
  • A flashlight with extra batteries
  • A first aid kit
  • Extra cash in small bills
  • Copies of important documents
  • A family meeting spot if you can’t get home
  • An out-of-state contact everyone checks in with

A family of 4 needs 56 gallons of water. A case of bottled water is about 3 gallons.
